# Sources, translations and commentaries

Śāstrālaya serves attributed editions, **not a certification of doctrinal authenticity**. A faithful copy of a digital transcription does not prove it perfectly reflects manuscripts or authorial intent.

## Source boundaries

- Each GRETIL-derived UTF-8 file is pinned by Git blob SHA-1 and byte size, then served in unchanged source order. File-specific upstream metadata is in `sources.json`. Licence: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; attribution and noncommercial ShareAlike conditions apply to the contributed transcriptions. See the GRETIL header for rights and takedown information.
- The 1900s English works are the complete Project Gutenberg e-books named by source ID. The downloadable original is preserved, not edited into a claimed literal translation.
- A Sanskrit text, its translation and its bhāṣya are distinct witnesses with potentially different numbering and theological commitments. The UI must label the source/tradition and never invent 1:1 alignment.
- Swami Paramananda's 1919 translation and commentary includes the Īśā, Kaṭha and Kena Upaniṣads (Project Gutenberg 3283). Direct links go to the corresponding exact heading line in the hosted source. They are **book-section companion links** only.
- The four-commentary Gītā file represents Śrīdhara, Madhusūdana, Viśvanātha and Baladeva; Rāmānuja's Bhagavadgītābhāṣya represents another named historical viewpoint. All are Sanskrit, not modern English translations. They remain distinct.
- The Īśopaniṣad source explicitly warns that its commentary ascribed to Śaṅkara **has not been proofread**. Show this on the landing page, not behind a generic verification badge.
- No AI-generated English translations are published as authoritative. No unidentified modern translation is republished. Our verification proves pin/hash, structural preservation and runtime availability; not historical textual infallibility.

## Future proper alignment

Build verse-by-verse Sanskrit and translation only after identifying the same Sanskrit recension, original printed translation witness, correspondence table, differing verse boundaries, footnotes/commentary blocks, source licence, and a reproducible comparison audit. Until then, translation companions open separately.
